# Product mission

## One-sentence definition

Global Builder Opportunity Radar discovers, verifies, deduplicates, and ranks public opportunities
that can generate income through projects, contributions, prizes, or contracts outside traditional
employment.

## Target opportunities

The product accepts:

- freelance assignments with a defined deliverable or hourly contract;
- fixed-price projects and short-term technical contracts;
- open-source bounties with verifiable payment terms;
- grants, fellowships, and builder funding open to individuals or small teams;
- hackathons and competitions with monetary or liquid-token prizes;
- paid contributor, ambassador, ecosystem, and developer programs.
- freelance or contract work managing marketing, sales, CRM, content, reporting, or customer
  operations where automation is a core deliverable or competitive advantage.

The product prioritizes opportunities compatible with Python, browser automation, scraping, APIs,
data extraction, integrations, AI agents, LLM workflows, and open-source engineering.

It also targets business-operations problems that can be solved with those capabilities:

- marketing operations and campaign workflow automation;
- CRM implementation, cleanup, enrichment, and integration;
- lead generation, qualification, and follow-up automation;
- email, content, and publication automation;
- Sales Ops, RevOps, analytics, and reporting dashboards;
- customer-support workflows and chatbots;
- n8n, Make, Zapier, and similar workflow projects.

Opportunity kind and service domain are separate dimensions. For example, a result can be a
`contract` whose service domains are `crm_automation` and `lead_generation`. Marketing manager
employment remains excluded.

## Explicit exclusions

- ordinary permanent or full-time employment listings;
- permanent marketing-manager, social-media-manager, sales-manager, or operations employment;
- commission-only sales arrangements without a defined paid deliverable;
- unpaid volunteer work, exposure-only work, or courses sold as opportunities;
- airdrops, speculative trading, gambling, referral spam, and pay-to-apply schemes;
- opportunities requiring private keys, seed phrases, deposits, purchases, or account rental;
- scraped contact lists without an associated real opportunity and public provenance;
- opportunities that exclude Brazil when eligibility is a hard requirement;
- automated outreach, application submission, or financial transactions in the core radar.

## Product promise

Every surfaced result should answer, when the source makes the information available:

1. What work or contribution is requested?
2. Who is offering it and where is the original evidence?
3. What is the compensation, prize, or funding mechanism?
4. What is the deadline and expected effort?
5. Can a resident of Brazil participate?
6. Which technologies and skills are required?
7. How well does it match the configured profile?
8. What action should the user take next?
9. Which technical or business service domains does it belong to?

Unknown information must remain unknown; the system never invents certainty.

## Success criteria

The radar is successful when it consistently produces a small actionable shortlist with fewer false
positives than manual browsing, preserves evidence for every result, and can run repeatedly without
duplicates or human repair.
